**The goal:** files the organization owns, that anyone on the team can find, and that stay put when staff, volunteers or board members leave.

## 1. Know the three places

| Location | Who owns the files | Who can access | Use it for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **My Drive** | You, individually | You and anyone granted access through sharing. Sharing should be for temporary situations. | Drafts and personal working files only. If you leave, an admin will need to transfer these files owned by your Workspace account. |
| **Shared drives** | The organization | Drive members and others given access to specific files or folders, subject to sharing restrictions | Everything the team needs: programs, finance, board, grants. Files stay in place when people leave. |
| **Shared with me** | Someone else | The File's Owner and others with permission to those files | A view of files others shared with you. Not a storage location. |

**Rule of thumb:** if anyone else will ever need a file, it belongs in a shared drive.

[Google Workspace for Nonprofits](https://support.google.com/nonprofits/answer/2858465?hl=en), available at no cost for eligible organizations, includes 100 TB of storage pooled across the organization. That pool covers Drive, Gmail and Google Photos—not just shared drives.

## 2. Set up shared drives

- **Organize by function, not by person.** A typical nonprofit needs 5–8 drives, for example: Administration & Finance, Board, Programs, Development & Grants, Communications, HR (restricted).
- **Give access with groups, not individuals.** Add groups such as board@yourorg.org to a drive. When someone joins or leaves the group, their access follows automatically.
- **Assign at least two Managers** per drive so no one person holds the keys.
- **Pick the lowest role that works:**
  - *Manager*: everything, including membership and deleting the drive
  - *Content manager*: add, edit, move and delete files
  - *Contributor*: add and edit files in Drive on the web, but can't delete or move existing shared-drive files. In Drive for desktop, this role is read-only; people who need to create or edit files there need Content manager or Manager access.
  - *Commenter / Viewer*: read only, with or without comments
- **Keep folders shallow**: two or three levels is plenty. Split sensitive material (salaries, donor data) into its own drive instead of hiding it in a deep folder.

Google's [shared-drive permissions guide](https://support.google.com/a/users/answer/12380484?hl=en) explains the roles and how sharing restrictions affect access.

## 3. Daily habits

- **Create files directly in the shared drive.** Don't start in My Drive and plan to move them later.
- **Name files consistently:** `YYYY-MM Topic Description` (e.g., "2026-11 Board Minutes"). Avoid "final", "v3" and "copy of".
- **Use version history (File > Version history) instead of saving duplicates** in Google Docs, Sheets and Slides.
- **Share links, not attachments**, so everyone works from one copy.
- **Use shortcuts (Organize > Add shortcut) instead of copies** when a file belongs in more than one place.
- **Search before you browse:** Drive search can filter by type, owner, people and date modified.
- **Install Drive for desktop** to work with shared drives from File Explorer or Finder. Give people the access level they need for desktop editing, as noted above.
- **Check external sharing:** share specific files or folders with partners, and avoid "Anyone with the link" for anything sensitive.

## 4. Migrate from a personal Gmail Drive

Many organizations start out with files in someone's personal @gmail.com account. **Google won't transfer ownership straight from a personal account to your organization's account.** One approach is for the owner to move files into a shared drive, which makes the organization their owner. Test this with a small batch first: account permissions and sharing policies can affect what moves and who retains access.

1. **Take inventory.** In the personal account, sort what belongs to the organization from what's personal. Note any files owned by *other* people: for this personal-account migration, ask each owner to move their files. Within Workspace, administrators can also allow Editors to move eligible files.
2. **Build the destination first.** Create the shared drives and top-level folders from Section 2.
3. **Open the door temporarily.** If existing settings don't already permit it, an admin allows external membership and sharing for the migration, then adds the personal Gmail account to the destination drive as a *Content manager*. Record the previous settings so they can be restored afterward.
4. **Move the files.** Sign in to the personal Gmail account (a separate browser profile helps). Move a few test files into the shared drive, then verify their contents, links and access from the organization's account before continuing. Move files rather than making copies to retain the original files and their history. If moving folders is blocked, recreate the folders in the shared drive and move the files in batches. Recheck access: permissions inherited from the original folders aren't carried over.
5. **Handle what won't move.** For files owned by others, ask the owner to move them, or make a copy. Copies get a new link and don't retain the original version history. For large or messy drives, plan and test a migration tool that supports the source and destination accounts. Google Takeout download and re-upload is another option, but it creates new files rather than preserving the originals' links and version history; review exported formats and any comments you need to retain before relying on it.
6. **Close the door.** Verify the organization can access and manage the files without the Gmail account. Remove that account from the shared drive, restore the previous external-sharing settings, and update any links on your website, forms or email signatures that pointed to copies.

See Google's [ownership-transfer restrictions](https://support.google.com/drive/answer/2494892?hl=en) and [shared-drive migration guidance](https://knowledge.workspace.google.com/admin/drive/move-your-organizations-content-to-shared-drives) before starting. A successful move and a download/re-upload are different operations; don't assume they preserve the same information.

## Quick checklist

- Shared drives created by function, each with two or more Managers
- Access granted through groups
- Naming convention shared with staff
- Organization files moved out of personal Gmail accounts
- External sharing settings reviewed and tightened
